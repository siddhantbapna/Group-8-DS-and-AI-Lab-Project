Welcome to the Brain Tumor Segmentation Tool!

This application leverages a powerful AI model to automatically identify, segment, and visualize different sub-regions of a brain tumor from a set of standard MRI scans.

The Workflow: A Simple 4-Step Process

  1. Load Data: Begin by providing the necessary medical images. You can use the "Upload Files" tab for your own data (in .nii or .nii.gz format) or select a sample case from the "Use Examples" tab to see a demonstration.
  2. Process Files: Once all four required scans are uploaded, this button becomes active. Clicking it performs crucial pre-processing steps, such as aligning all scans to a common orientation (co-registration) and standardizing their intensity values (normalization). This ensures the AI model receives clean, consistent data.
  3. Run Prediction: This is where the AI performs its analysis. The model examines the processed scans and generates a detailed, voxel-by-voxel map of the predicted tumor regions.
  4. Explore & Analyze: After the prediction is complete, you can use the viewer tabs to explore the results visually and quantitatively.

Exploring the Results

  • 2D Slice Viewer: This is the classic way to view MRI data, showing one cross-sectional "slice" of the brain at a time. Use the slider to move through the brain from top to bottom.
    • Modalities Tab: Displays the original four MRI scans you uploaded. This is useful for seeing the raw data the AI used.
    • Prediction Tab: Shows the AI's output. You can see the individual tumor components (WT, TC, ET) separately or as a combined "Prediction Overlay" on the T1ce scan for anatomical context.
    • Ground Truth Tab: This tab only appears if you upload an optional "Ground Truth" file. It shows the segmentation drawn by a human expert, allowing for a direct visual comparison with the AI's prediction.
  • 3D Volume Viewer: This powerful view reconstructs the 2D slices into a fully interactive 3D model, helping you understand the tumor's true shape, size, and location.
    • Interaction: Click and drag with the left mouse button to rotate the model. Use the mouse wheel to zoom in and out. Right-click and drag to pan the model across the screen.
    • Controls & Comparison: Use the checkboxes on the side panels to toggle the visibility of the brain surface and each tumor component. If a ground truth file is loaded, you'll see a split-screen view for a direct side-by-side comparison of the 3D prediction and the expert's segmentation.
  • Analysis Tab: This provides the numbers behind the visuals, offering a quantitative comparison between the prediction and ground truth.
    • Volume (in voxels): This metric tells you the size of each tumor component. A "voxel" is a 3D pixel, the basic unit of an MRI scan.
    • Dice Score: This is a standard metric in medical imaging that measures the accuracy of a prediction. It scores the overlap between the AI's prediction and the ground truth on a scale from 0 (no overlap) to 1 (a perfect match). A higher score means a more accurate prediction.

Understanding the MRI Scans (Modalities)

Each MRI scan provides a unique view of the brain tissue, and all four are necessary for an accurate segmentation:

  • T1ce (Post-contrast T1-weighted): A contrast agent is injected into the patient. This agent "lights up" areas where the blood-brain barrier is compromised, which is characteristic of the most aggressive parts of a high-grade tumor. This scan is essential for identifying the Enhancing Tumor.
  • T1 (Pre-contrast T1-weighted): Provides a clear anatomical map of the brain, showing sharp boundaries between different tissue types.
  • FLAIR (Fluid-Attenuated Inversion Recovery): A special type of scan that cancels out the signal from fluids (like cerebrospinal fluid). This makes it exceptionally good at visualizing edema—the swelling and fluid buildup in the tissue surrounding the tumor.
  • T2 (T2-weighted): This scan is also sensitive to water content and is useful for highlighting both the edema and other parts of the tumor core that may not be visible on other scans.

Understanding the Tumor Segmentation Labels

The AI model identifies three distinct, nested sub-regions of the tumor, which have clinical significance:

  • Whole Tumor (WT - Red): This is the largest region, representing the entire area of abnormal tissue. It includes the solid part of the tumor plus all the surrounding edema identified on the FLAIR scan.
  • Tumor Core (TC - Green): This is the solid portion of the tumor, located *inside* the Whole Tumor. It excludes the surrounding edema.
  • Enhancing Tumor (ET - Blue/Yellow): This is the most active and aggressive part of the tumor, located *inside* the Tumor Core. This is the region identified on the T1ce scan.
